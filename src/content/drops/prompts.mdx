Most people get bad output from AI tools for one reason: they ask vague
questions and expect specific answers.

"Help me with my code" and "write me a caption" are requests, not prompts. A
prompt is a request **plus** the context, constraints, and format that let the
model actually do the job well. The model isn't withholding a better answer;
it doesn't have enough to work with, so it gives you the average of everything
it's seen, which is exactly what a generic answer is.

## The four-part anatomy

Every good prompt below is built from the same four parts. Once you can see
them, you can fix any prompt that isn't working by asking which part is missing.

1. **Context:** what's actually going on: your situation, your level, your
   constraints. This is the part people skip, and it's the part that does the
   most work.
2. **Task:** the specific thing you want done, not the general area. "Review
   this" is an area. "Flag anything that'll bite me in 6 months" is a task.
3. **Constraints:** what to prioritize, avoid, or rank by. Without these the
   model optimises for sounding helpful, which is not the same as being useful.
4. **Format:** how you want the answer shaped, so you're not editing structure
   after the fact.

You'll see that pattern repeat 100 times below. The repetition is the point:
once you've internalised the shape, you stop needing a list like this at all.

## The one meta-skill

Every prompt above is a variation on the same four moves: give real context, ask
for something specific, tell it what to prioritize, and shape the output format.
Once that's automatic you don't need a document like this any more; you'll write
prompts that way by default.

If you want to go further than prompting: the same discipline applied to a build
rather than a chat window is [the system design template](/drops/system): decide
