SEO (search engine optimisation) gets your pages ranked in a list of search
results. AEO (answer engine optimisation) gets your content picked as the direct
answer to a question. GEO (generative engine optimisation) gets your business
named inside the answers AI tools like ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity write.
They share the same foundation and differ in the goal: a click, a quote or a
mention.

## What is SEO?

Search engine optimisation is the old, familiar one: making your pages rank
high in Google and Bing for the words people type. Success is a position in a
list, and the reward is a click to your site.

It's built on pages search engines can crawl, content that matches what people
search for, a fast site, and links from other sites that vouch for you.

## What is AEO?

Answer engine optimisation is about the moment the search engine stops showing
a list and just answers: featured snippets, "People also ask" boxes, voice
assistants and Google's AI Overviews.

Success is your words being the answer, usually with your site credited. The
work is mostly about how a page is written:

- A question as the heading, in the words people actually ask it.
- A direct answer in the first sentence or two under it.
- Then the detail, tables and steps for the person who wants more.
- FAQ and HowTo structured data where it fits.

## What is GEO?

Generative engine optimisation is about the answers AI assistants write from
scratch. When someone asks ChatGPT "which CRM should a five-person agency use?",
it writes a paragraph and names a few products. GEO is the work of being one of
them.

Success is a mention, often with no link at all. AI tools name businesses that
they can read about clearly and that many sources describe consistently, so GEO
leans harder than SEO on what other sites say about you: reviews, directories,
comparison articles, community answers.

## What do GEO, AEO and SEO have in common?

More than the acronyms suggest. All three fail the same way if the foundation is
missing:

- **Crawlers can't read the page.** If your content only appears after
  JavaScript runs, many crawlers see an empty page, and AI crawlers are the
  worst at this. [How to check yours](/fixes/lovable-app-invisible-to-google-and-chatgpt).
- **robots.txt blocks them.** A rule meant for one bot often blocks the AI
  search crawlers too.
- **Nothing says who you are.** Structured data for your organisation and what
  you sell removes guesswork for all three.
- **Pages are vague.** A page that answers one question plainly does well in
  every one of them.

ten seconds.

## Which one does my business need?

Look at how your customers decide:

| If your customers mostly… | Prioritise | Measure it by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Search Google and compare a few sites | SEO | Rankings and organic clicks |
| Ask quick factual questions about your field | AEO | Featured snippets and AI Overview citations |
| Ask an AI assistant to recommend someone | GEO | Mentions across a fixed set of AI questions |

Most businesses need the foundation plus one of the others, not three separate
projects. And if an agency is selling GEO without first checking whether AI
crawlers can read your site at all, start there yourself.
