If your app already complies with GDPR, most of the engineering carries over to
India's DPDP Act: notices, consent records, access and deletion, retention and
vendor contracts. Three things differ enough to need new work: DPDP has no risk
threshold for breach reporting, it treats everyone under 18 as a child, and it
relies on consent far more than GDPR's six legal bases do.

This is a developer's comparison of what the product has to do. It isn't legal
advice.

## What carries over from GDPR to DPDP?

The good news first. If you built these for GDPR, keep them:

- **Notices and consent records.** An itemised notice, consent captured per
  purpose, and a log of who agreed to what, when.
- **Data subject requests.** Export, correction and deletion flows. DPDP calls
  them rights of the Data Principal, but the product work is the same.
- **Retention and erasure jobs.** Deleting data once its purpose is served.
- **Security controls.** Encryption, access control and logging.
- **Processor contracts.** Agreements with every vendor that handles personal
  data for you.

## Where is DPDP stricter than GDPR?

| | GDPR | DPDP |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Breach reporting | To the regulator within 72 hours, unless the breach is unlikely to risk people's rights | Every personal data breach: affected people without delay, and the Data Protection Board with a detailed report within 72 hours |
| Children | Under 16, which member states can lower to 13 | Under 18, with verifiable parental consent |
| Tracking minors | No outright ban; extra care and age-appropriate design expected | Tracking, behavioural monitoring and targeted ads at children are prohibited |
| Access logs | Not set by the law | Logs kept for at least a year under the Rules |
| Maximum penalty | Up to 4% of global turnover or €20 million | Up to ₹250 crore, decided by the Board case by case |

The breach row is the one that catches teams out. A GDPR process that triages
"is this reportable?" needs a different default for Indian users: assume yes.

## Where is DPDP narrower than GDPR?

- **Legal bases.** GDPR has six, including legitimate interests, which a lot of
  analytics and marketing rests on. DPDP rests almost everything on consent, with
  a short list of "legitimate uses" (such as data a person volunteers for a
  specific purpose, or legal and employment needs). If your GDPR setup leans on
  legitimate interests, expect to ask for consent instead.
- **Scope.** DPDP covers digital personal data, and personal data in non-digital
  form only once it's digitised.

## What should I build first?

1. **Breach reporting with no threshold** for Indian users, and logging that
   lets you write the 72-hour report.
2. **An 18+ age gate** and a parental consent flow if minors can sign up.
3. **A consent review** of everything you currently justify as legitimate
   interest: analytics, marketing, personalisation.
4. **Log retention** of at least a year if you keep less today.
