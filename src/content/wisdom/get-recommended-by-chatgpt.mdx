To get your business recommended by ChatGPT, you need three things: AI crawlers
must be able to read your website, your pages must say plainly who you are, what
you sell and for whom, and other trusted websites must mention you in the same
terms. ChatGPT recommends what it can read about and confirm. Fix your own site
first, then earn the mentions.

That's the whole answer. The rest of this page is how to do each part.

## How does ChatGPT decide which businesses to recommend?

When someone asks "best organic skincare brand in India", ChatGPT (and Gemini,
and Perplexity) doesn't look up a ranking. It builds an answer from what it
already learned and, more and more often, from a live web search it runs for
that question. Then it names the few businesses that come up clearly and
consistently in what it read.

So it isn't one thing. It's two questions the AI is effectively asking:

- **Can I read about this business?** If your site doesn't load for its
  crawler, or loads as an empty page, you don't exist to it.
- **Does what I read agree?** If your site says "natural skincare", a directory
  says "beauty products" and nothing else mentions you, the AI has nothing solid
  to repeat. A business described the same way in many places is an easy thing
  to recommend.

## How do I check whether ChatGPT recommends my business?

Ask it. Seriously, that's the first step, and most businesses have never done
it properly.

1. **Write ten questions your customers actually ask.** "Best [your category]
   in [your city]", "[your category] for [a specific need]", "is [your brand]
   any good?", "alternatives to [the competitor everyone knows]".
2. **Ask each one in ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity.** Use a logged-out or fresh
   chat so your history doesn't steer it.
3. **Write down who gets named** each time, and whether you do.

That table is your baseline. Keep it, because the only honest way to know
whether anything you change is working is to ask the same ten questions again
in a month.

## Why can't AI read my website?

This is the part most businesses get wrong without knowing it, and it's the
easiest to fix.

- **The content isn't in the HTML.** Many modern sites, including most apps
  built with Lovable, Bolt or a plain React setup, send the browser an almost
  empty page and fill it in with JavaScript. People see a full page. Most AI
  crawlers don't run JavaScript, so they see the empty one. [Here's how to check
  and fix it](/fixes/lovable-app-invisible-to-google-and-chatgpt).
- **robots.txt blocks AI crawlers.** Some site builders and security plugins
  block bots like GPTBot, OAI-SearchBot or PerplexityBot by default. Blocking
  the search ones keeps you out of their answers.
- **There's no structured data.** A few lines of markup (Organization or
  LocalBusiness, Product or Service, FAQ) let AI state who you are and what you
  sell without guessing.

## What should my pages say?

Write for the question, not for a slogan. AI tools repeat specific, plain
statements, and they skip vague ones.

- Say what you sell and who it's for in the first lines of the page: "Organic,
  cruelty-free skincare made in Jaipur, shipped across India" beats "Beauty,
  reimagined".
- Give the facts a buyer asks about their own page: prices, sizes, delivery
  areas, returns, opening hours, what's included.
- Answer the common questions on the page itself, in a short FAQ, in the words
  customers use.

## How do I get mentioned on other sites?

This is the slow part, and it matters most for competitive categories. AI
trusts a business more when independent sources describe it.

- **Directories and profiles:** Google Business Profile, the directories in your
  industry, marketplaces you sell on. Same name, same description, everywhere.
- **Reviews:** on the platforms people in your category actually use.
- **Lists and comparisons:** "best [category] in [city]" articles are exactly
  what AI search reads for "best [category] in [city]" questions.
- **Community answers:** genuinely helpful replies on Reddit, Quora and
  industry forums, where your business is the honest answer.

## Is this the same as SEO?

It overlaps, and good SEO helps. But SEO is about ranking in a list of links,
and this is about being named in a single answer. [The difference between GEO,

what to spend on.
